# Central data and consent boundaries

ReMatch is a local synthetic prototype and a trusted central processor. The backend can decrypt authorized clinical records to run deterministic screening. The matcher does not need to decrypt identity records. This replaces the historical clinic federation and ciphertext-relay design.

A fresh central-v1 SQLite database stores encrypted identity/contact, FHIR-shaped clinical history, matching evidence/history, optional discussion notes and exact introduction snapshots. AES-256-GCM uses a unique random nonce and authenticated data containing the central ownership domain, patient ID, record type and schema version. Wrong patient, type, key or tampered ciphertext fails authentication. Central keys and per-principal API tokens are generated outside SQLite in ignored local key files; keys, .env and runtime files must not be distributed.

Patient and doctor APIs resolve the caller from a principal-bound secret, rather than accepting an arbitrary patient header. A patient can access their own profile. Doctor access is restricted to that patient's assigned doctor and relevant discussion cases. Research teams can access their own listings and specifically authorized introductions. There is no team patient-directory or candidate endpoint.

Screening, simulated record access, discussion outcome, introduction authorization and enrollment outcome are separate concepts. Imports require both screening and record-access permissions. Low-risk direct discovery requires explicit source support. Higher/unknown risk requires a current assigned-doctor discussion before preview or introduction. Discussion does not substitute for patient permission.

A disclosure preview contains exactly name, selected contact and a relevant screening summary; it identifies one PI/site, purpose, study version and record version. Authorization is tied to that patient, preview, recipient and current match/permission revisions. Previews expire after 15 minutes and after a demo-day change. Delivery is idempotent by authorization ID. A changed study/source, clinical import, or permission invalidates pending previews; changed records never automatically expand a previously shared packet.

No patient information is submitted to the LLM. Public registry/synthetic source retrieval is separate from patient storage, and prompts are constructed from an allow-list of source fields. Brief claims are exact source quotes and locations. Audit and outbox rows retain actor/action/reference/state metadata, not copied identity, records, notes, API keys or tokens.

Disconnect blocks subsequent imports. Stop matching revokes introductions, records in-app retractions, removes retained clinical bundles and derived match/preview data, and closes current tasks. Identity remains for the usable profile until separate deletion. Delete removes identity, clinical histories and derived payloads, including stored introduction packets and discussion notes, retaining only minimal pseudonymous state/audit references and earned demo credit. Outbox retractions are persisted and retried on startup or delivery processing. Software cannot erase a recipient's prior knowledge.

Limits: role switching and locally stored credentials simulate accounts, not production authentication. File permissions and OS access are outside these application checks; a process holding the central key can decrypt retained data. SQLite metadata reveals pseudonymous IDs, workflow states and relationships. Logical deletion is not a forensic disk-erasure guarantee. In-app retraction is not an external recall guarantee. No HIPAA, IRB, Epic, clinical-safety or production retention compliance is claimed.
